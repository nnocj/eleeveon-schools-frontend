Phase 2 — Unified identity, staff access and portal routing
All users will have one Eleeveon email/password identity.

The central backend will manage:
- Accounts, schools and branches.
- Owner, Branch Admin and Teacher identities.
- Class and subject identifiers.
- Staff assignments and access permissions.
- School package and deployment type.
- Authorized devices and login sessions.
- Password resets, staff deactivation and revocation.
- Protected offline access after initial provisioning.
A person belonging to multiple schools should be able to select the appropriate school after signing in.

Essentials: The school can maintain teacher records and assignments, but teachers do not receive Teacher Portal access.

Multi-device: Teachers receive portal access based on their active memberships, assignments and entitlements.

Acceptance gate: Unauthorized users cannot open a portal or retrieve another school's data, even by calling backend endpoints directly.
Phase 3 — Licensing, activation and Google Drive provisioning
This phase connects Independent software to Eleeveon's managed infrastructure.

Licensing
- Preserve existing paid Essentials licenses and certificates.
- Automatically discover purchased entitlements.
- Support one-time Essentials and Multi-device purchases.
- Support package upgrades without deleting school data.
- Distinguish perpetual software rights from renewable services.
- Maintain Eleeveon Hub, support requests and remote licensing functions.
Google Drive
- Owner connects a school-controlled Google account.
- Backend completes secure Google OAuth authorization.
- Store refresh tokens encrypted.
- Create or select the managed school storage location.
- Initialize manifests, encryption metadata and backup structures.
- Display connection status, quota-related failures and recovery options.
- Allow authorized reconnection and disconnection.
The school must also have a documented way to export its data and recover from a disconnected or unavailable Google account.

Acceptance gate: An Essentials school can provision Drive without changing its license, and one school cannot access another school's Drive connection.

Phase 5 — Independent Multi-device synchronization
This is the largest technical phase.

The dedicated Independent backend will coordinate multiple staff devices without moving the school's permanent operational database into Eleeveon's Cloud database.

Synchronization engine
- Initial device synchronization.
- Incremental record downloads and uploads.
- Offline mutation queues.
- Change manifests and version tracking.
- Idempotent operations and retry handling.
- Concurrent-edit detection and conflict resolution.
- Deleted-record synchronization and tombstone retention.
- Media upload, download and local caching.
- Bandwidth-efficient batching and compression.
- Sync status, diagnostics and repair workflows.
Role-specific access

Owners and Branch Admins access their permitted school/branch information. Teachers access assigned classes, subjects, assessments and relevant students only.

The gateway must enforce this server-side, including after a teacher's assignment changes or employment ends.

Security design

Before implementation, we will define encryption boundaries so the gateway can authorize individual records without unnecessarily exposing school data. Google Drive file revisions alone are not sufficient for safe multi-device concurrency.

Acceptance gate: Two teachers can work offline on separate devices, reconnect in different orders and synchronize without losing valid changes or exposing unauthorized records.

5. Development delivery arrangement
To keep implementation manageable, each phase will have at most two substantial packages.
Delivery	Contents
Backend drop-in	NestJS modules, Prisma changes, migrations, tests and configuration instructions
Frontend drop-in	Next.js/Electron integration, portal updates, local storage integration and UI changes

Some phases may need only one package. Each delivery should include an installation guide, file-change manifest, environment variable names, verification commands and rollback notes.

We will not assume a stage is complete just because TypeScript compiles or the application deploys.
